v4, 2026-11-14. A Labyrinth-style walk to the goal, built from the game's seed: the same seed gives the same maze and the same rounds. Change the seed to see another.

How it plays

  • Each round deals every player their plays: a few small pegging scores (dashed) and their match turn's sets. Each play is worth steps and carries a suit, or none on a tie.
  • Players take turns making plays, as in pegging: the seat after the dealer goes first, and the deal passes each round. A player with no plays left says go; the others keep playing. The first to the goal wins.
  • On your turn, tap a play to walk it. You must take every step. The token walks along corridors and stops at forks for you to pick a way (the pulsing squares).
  • Suit gates guard shortcuts. Only steps of that suit pass; anything else bounces off the gate (costing a step) and walks back the way it came.
  • Doors on the main route absorb steps. Damage stays; once a door is down it stays open.
  • Prize cells (stars) sit at dead ends, like Labyrinth's treasures. They give items: a jump ahead, a key for any gate, or extra steps.
  • The main route is exactly the game's length. Shortcuts make it shorter if you have the suit.

What's real and what's a stand-in

  • Real: the dice (dice.js, the engine's streams), the counting (scoring.js) and each set's suit.
  • Stand-in: the sets. Each round shuffles the deck (stream deal) and takes the best set of 2 to 5 from each run of 7 cards. That's well below a real match turn (a medium bot scores about 243 steps a turn at r15), so try 8 sets a round to feel the real scale.
  • Stand-in: pegging is 2 or 3 small scores from single cards, not a real play.
  • Bots: they make their biggest play first, take the shortest way at forks (through gates their suit or a key opens), and use items at once. Bot colours are proposals (see the tiles style guide).
  • Not yet: a full round's other stages. Shifting walls are removed for now, so the route is always the game's length.

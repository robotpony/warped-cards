v1, 2026-11-17. Pegging on the maze and the maze walk as one turn-based board game, built from the game's seed. A proposal to react to: every rule here is a starting guess, and the settings below try the alternatives.

How a hand plays

  • Grid results. Each player's scoring grid becomes maze points: their grid steps divided by 40 (setting), kept by suit as the sets were (the grid is a stand-in here: random totals near the setting). A set's points carry its suit; a tie carries none.
  • Pegging. Real pegging to 31 with a 4-card hand each (the deal is a stand-in: no discard or crib). Every point moves the scorer's token at once, one step a point, carrying the suit of the card that scored. You must take every step: on a ring you pick which way to turn, and at a fork which way to go.
  • Maze turns. Then players take turns, the seat after the dealer first. A turn is one action, spending up to 6 maze points (setting): move (tap a lit cell), turn a ring one notch either way (tokens and prizes ride with it, and its openings line up with new cells), close a door on any open passage no token stands beside, or play a joker. Say go to sit out the rest of the hand. Unspent points are lost (setting).
  • The board. Rings of cells around the goal, with walls splitting each ring into arcs. Every arc has a way inward, so the goal is always reachable however the rings turn. Openings inward can be suit gates (a point of that suit, a key, or 3 more points) or doors (spend their strength to break them; damage stays and a broken door is open for everyone). Stars give jokers when you stop on them.
  • Jokers are the grid's power jokers with maze uses: the swapper swaps places with any token, the bomb blows open a wall beside you, the burn burns away any door, the reach adds 4 maze points now, and a key (from stars) opens a gate. Everyone starts with one bomb. A pair that swapped can't swap back in the same hand.
  • The first token into the centre wins.

The bots

  • Pegging: the card that scores most now; on a tie, the lowest card.
  • Maze turns: each action is scored as its cut to the bot's cost to the goal, plus half of what it adds to the leader's cost, less the points it spends. It moves when nothing beats moving, closes doors in front of a leader that's close, turns rings when a better opening lines up, and swaps with a leader far ahead.

Open questions for Bruce

  1. Do maze turns come after pegging (as here), or interleave with it, a maze turn after each go?
  2. Should grid points move you at all, or only open gates, turn rings and close doors, leaving movement to pegging?
  3. Is closing a door anywhere too strong? Try "near your token only" next.
  4. Ring turns: any ring, or only rings you stand on?
  5. Should the outer ring be a start lane where everyone begins, or one start cell, as now?

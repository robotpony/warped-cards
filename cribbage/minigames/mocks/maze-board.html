How a hand plays

  • The scoring grid comes first. Every set you score on the grid, and your hand and crib counts from the show, becomes a play: its cards, its steps and its suit (the most common suit; a tie has none). The grid here is a recorded medium-bot turn for each player: about 14 plays and 226 steps a hand.
  • Pegging is the maze. Players take turns, the seat after the dealer first, until every play is used. On your turn, pick a play: the board shows where it will take you. Then walk it, or spend it to close a door (the door takes the play's suit, and its steps as strength), or to flip a switch. You can close one door a hand (setting). Before that you can play one joker.
  • Walking. One step a point, and every step is taken. Walks go forward: they never turn into a dead end or a pocket. Where two ways both lead on, a junction (a gold disc with an arrow, at real forks near the tokens and along a previewed path) decides: you go the way it points, and it turns to the other way for whoever comes next, so arriving first or second matters. One way is often a loop that circles back.
  • Doors have a suit and a strength, and you need both: only a play of the door's suit wears it down, and the steps spent on it don't move you. Damage stays; a broken door is open for everyone. A play of another suit stops at the door, and the rest of it is lost. Suit gates pass their suit only; another suit bounces off (one step) and walks back. Switches are a pair of passages, one open and one shut; flipping swaps them, for everyone. Each switch flips once a hand.
  • Stars sit on the loops and give a joker when a play ends on them: key (one wrong-suit gate or door), burn (break any door), bomb (a wall beside you; a big shortcut leaves a door of half the cut, no suit), swapper (swap places with a token, not back the same hand).
  • The first token into the goal wins. The target is 6 to 12 hands a game, like cribbage.

Bot styles

  • Balanced: looks ahead: walks the play that leaves it cheapest to the goal; closes a door in front of a leader who is ahead and within 200 of the goal; flips a switch or plays a joker when it pays.
  • Racer: its biggest play first, always walks.
  • Timer: looks ahead like balanced, but never closes doors or flips switches: it only picks the play and its timing at junctions.
  • Door closer: closes a door in front of anyone level with it or ahead.
  • Jokers: plays every joker as soon as it helps at all, and flips switches eagerly.

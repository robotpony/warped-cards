v2, 2026-11-17. Pegging on the maze and the maze walk as one turn-based board game, on maze-walk's grid maze (v4) in the neon look (design/neon/neon-maze.js). The same seed gives the same maze and the same hands. Every rule here is a starting guess; the settings try the alternatives.

How a hand plays

  • Grid results. Each player's scoring grid becomes maze points: grid steps ÷ 40 (setting), kept by suit as the sets were. The grid is a stand-in: random totals near the setting. A tie carries no suit.
  • Pegging. Real pegging to 31 with 4 cards each (a stand-in deal, no discard or crib). Every point walks the scorer one step at once, carrying the suit of the card that scored. You must take every step and pick the way at each fork. A gate passes only its suit; other steps bounce off it. Doors soak up steps until they break.
  • Maze turns. Then players take turns, the seat after the dealer first. A turn is one action, spending up to 6 points (setting):
    • Move: tap a lit cell; each passage costs 1. A gate costs a point of its suit, or 3 more. A door costs its strength left plus 1. Tap a door's badge to spend what's left on it.
    • Flip a switch: each switch is a pair of passages, one open and one shut. Flipping swaps them, for everyone.
    • Close a door on an open passage near you.
    • Play a joker.
    Say go to sit out the rest of the hand. Unspent points are lost (setting).
  • The maze is maze-walk's: a seeded grid maze with a main route of exactly the game's length, loops that make forks, gated shortcuts, and doors on the route that get stronger as you go. Gated shortcuts save at most a fifth of the route. Stars sit at dead ends. Switches never cut a cell off from the goal, whichever way they're set, and never shorten the route by more than a fifth.
  • Jokers come from stars: jump 4 cells along the cheapest way, over gates and doors; key opens one gate; burn any door you can see; bomb a wall beside you (a cut of more than 4 steps leaves a door of half the cut); swapper swaps places with any token (not back in the same hand); reach adds 4 points now. Everyone starts with a bomb.
  • The first token into the goal wins. The plan is 5 to 13 hands a game (concept.md).

The bots

  • Pegging: the card that scores most now; on a tie, the lowest. At forks, the cheapest way to the goal.
  • Maze turns: each action is valued at its cut to the bot's cost to the goal, plus half of what it adds to the leader's, less the points spent. Moving is the default. They flip a switch or close a door when it pays better than moving. They swap with a leader far ahead.

Open questions

  1. Do maze turns come after all the pegging (as here), or does each player get a maze turn after each go?
  2. Should grid points also move you, or only pay for gates, switches and doors, leaving movement to pegging?
  3. Close a door near you (as here) or anywhere?
  4. Flip any switch from anywhere (as here), or only one you can see or stand beside?

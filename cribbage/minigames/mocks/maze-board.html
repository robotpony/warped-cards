How a hand plays

  • The scoring grid comes first. Every set you score on the grid, and your hand and crib counts, becomes a play: its cards, its steps and its suit (the most common suit; a tie has none). About 14 plays and 226 steps a hand.
  • Pegging is the maze. Players take turns until every play is used. Tap a card in your fan: the board shows where it goes, and the sheet offers Walk, Close a door (it takes the play's suit and steps) or Flip a switch. One joker a turn, before or after the play.
  • The maze is one golden path with dead ends off it, and a few variants: a way round each golden door (a door of another suit, or a longer free way) and shortcuts behind a suited door. No little loops.
  • Walking. One step a point. Walks go forward, never into dead ends, and avoid a door they can't open when there's another way. At a junction you go the way its arrow points, and it turns for whoever comes next.
  • Doors are cards across the path: rank is the strength left, suit is the suit they need. Only that suit wears them down, and those steps don't move you; another suit stops there. Damage stays.
  • Stuck? A player who ends a hand no closer to the goal makes the maze stir for them. Two stuck players duel: classic pegging with their kept hands, and the winner's door breaks and they take the loser's biggest play. Ending a walk on another player's cell starts a duel too.
  • Jokers are the scoring grid's power jokers, one set for the game. On the maze: swapper trades places, bomb opens a thin wall, reach lets your next play pick its ways at junctions (tap the ringed ones), burn sets a fire that burns the next token's plays, and the key takes one play past a wrong-suit door. Stars give more. The first token into the goal wins; the target is 6 to 12 hands.

Bot styles

  • Balanced: looks ahead: walks the play that leaves it cheapest to the goal; closes a door in front of a leader who is ahead and within 200 of the goal; flips a switch or plays a joker when it pays.
  • Racer: its biggest play first, always walks.
  • Timer: looks ahead like balanced, but never closes doors or flips switches: it only picks the play and its timing at junctions.
  • Door closer: closes a door in front of anyone level with it or ahead.
  • Jokers: plays every joker as soon as it helps at all, and flips switches eagerly.
